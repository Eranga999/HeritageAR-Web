The research behind HeritageAR

Offline-first mobile augmented reality for cultural heritage.

Full research titleHeritageAR: A Fully Offline Intelligent Augmented Reality Framework for Fine-Grained Landmark Recognition, Information Augmentation, On-Device RAG Conversational Assistance, and Visual Landmark-Based Navigation

01

Literature Review

Mobile augmented reality is well studied for heritage, but reported systems are usually judged on user experience rather than on the reliability of perception, and offline operation is rarely a design requirement.

AR for cultural heritage

Surveys identify visualisation, reconstruction, storytelling and guidance as the dominant uses. Recent reviews of storytelling patterns and of design and evaluation practice note that systems are mostly evaluated on user experience, not perception reliability, and offline operation is seldom treated as a requirement.

Cited works
  • Bekele et al.
  • Nikolarakis and Koutsabasis
  • Chatsiopoulou and Michailidis

Information presentation in mobile AR

View management decides where a label can be drawn without overlapping others or hiding the object. Outdoor studies show text legibility depends strongly on background texture and light, both difficult on rough rock under equatorial sun. Heritage systems usually avoid this by using fiducial markers or geographic anchors with pre-authored content.

Cited works
  • Bell, Feiner and Hollerer
  • Gabbard, Swan and Hix

On-device landmark recognition

Single-stage YOLO detectors, mobile backbones and integer-only quantisation allow real-time inference on limited hardware via TensorFlow Lite. Heritage recognition differs from generic detection: classes are fine-grained and share materials, so the main risk is confidently confusing one structure with another.

Cited works
  • Redmon et al.
  • Jocher et al.
  • Howard et al.
  • Jacob et al.
  • TensorFlow Lite

RAG, compact models and navigation without GNSS

Retrieval-augmented generation with sentence encoders, FAISS or HNSW indexes and compact local language models makes grounded on-device answers possible, which matters because unsubstantiated history sounds convincing. For positioning, visual odometry and SLAM are accurate but costly in battery and heat, so a lighter route-progress approach is preferred.

Cited works
  • Lewis et al.
  • Karpukhin et al.
  • Reimers and Gurevych
  • Abdin et al.
  • Scaramuzza and Fraundorfer
  • Mur-Artal and Tardos
02

Research Gap

No reviewed heritage guide performs recognition, grounded explanation, conversational question answering and in-site navigation entirely on the device, without a network connection.

In the reviewed studies these four capabilities are treated separately and the intelligent parts are mostly cloud-hosted. No heritage navigation method was found that is designed for situations where GNSS error is larger than the path granularity.

03

Problem

Heritage sites in South Asia are often located where mobile signal is weak or absent, so cloud-based guides fail exactly when visitors need them. Visitors need to know what they are looking at, its history and meaning, how to reach the next point and answers to follow-up questions that a signboard cannot give.

  1. 01

    Connectivity

    Rock corridors, terraces and tree cover weaken or remove mobile signal, so cloud inference is unreliable.

  2. 02

    Static content

    Marker- or location-bound content is fixed at build time and cannot adapt to visitor questions.

  3. 03

    Positioning error

    Consumer GNSS is off by several metres, and some visitor path legs at the site are about one metre long, so coordinate-based navigation cannot tell which segment the user is on.

Also addressed: language barriers on signboards (Sinhala, Tamil, English) and visitor safety near elephant and Asian giant honeybee warning areas.

04

Objectives

Main objective

Design, build and evaluate a fully offline intelligent AR heritage guide that combines fine-grained landmark recognition, grounded explanation, conversational assistance and GNSS-independent navigation, tested at Sigiriya.

  1. SO 01

    Build an offline architecture with four local components that makes no network request at runtime.

  2. SO 02

    Train and quantise a detector for specific heritage sub-landmarks and add geometric and sub-landmark filters that reject confident false detections without retraining.

  3. SO 03

    Create a detection-driven AR overlay that anchors content to the recognised structure and shows uncertainty instead of hiding it.

  4. SO 04

    Implement an on-device RAG assistant supporting both system-initiated explanations and visitor questions from one index and one local model.

  5. SO 05

    Develop a relative route navigation method using segment lengths, turn instructions and visually confirmed waypoints, with an admin survey mode for authoring routes in the field.

  6. SO 06

    Provide an offline map with wildlife warning areas and a background alert service that notifies visitors near elephant and Asian giant honeybee zones, with local-only location processing.

  7. SO 07

    Provide offline AR signboard translation with reliable Sinhala, Tamil and English OCR, heritage phrase matching and confidence-scored translation.

  8. SO 08

    Evaluate the system at Sigiriya in airplane mode for recognition, overlay stability, answer grounding, navigation task completion and latency.

05

Methodology

Design constraints

  • C1

    No component may make a network request at runtime, including telemetry, model download or content updates.

  • C2

    The whole deployable (app, detector, embedding model, vector index, knowledge base, language model) must fit a storage budget a visitor would reasonably allow.

  • C3

    Responsiveness must stay good on mid-range phones, not only flagships.

Pipeline

  1. 1
    Data and training

    Purpose-built Sigiriya landmark and sub-landmark dataset, photographed from varied viewpoints, distances, times of day and weather; YOLOv8n trained with Ultralytics.

  2. 2
    Quantise and deploy

    Export to TensorFlow Lite with full INT8 post-training quantisation; ship FP32 and INT8 and pick at start-up by delegate availability; inference runs in a background isolate.

  3. 3
    Filter and confirm

    Geometric plausibility and site-lock filters, then confidence threshold held over consecutive frames before a label is released.

  4. 4
    Overlay

    Confirmed, tentative and null states; content anchored to the confirmed structure; presentation decoupled from generation.

  5. 5
    Retrieve and generate

    Passage embeddings, cosine similarity, similarity floor, top-k context, local language model; one pipeline for system-initiated and visitor-initiated queries.

  6. 6
    Navigate

    Relative route of legs with displacement estimation and visual waypoint confirmation; routes authored in an admin survey mode.

  7. 7
    Map, alerts and translation

    Bundled GeoPackage offline map; geofence monitor with approach, inside and leaving states plus exit hysteresis; background local notifications; on-device OCR, heritage phrase matching, offline translation and dictionary fallback with confidence scoring.

  8. 8
    Field evaluation

    All tests at Sigiriya with the phone in airplane mode.

Evaluation protocol

Per-class precision, recall, F1, mAP@0.5, confusion matrix, FP32 vs INT8 accuracy, latency and size.

Anchoring stability and legibility during passes in front of structures, measured during the same field visits.

A set of factual, explanatory and out-of-scope questions scored for groundedness and refusal behaviour.

Task completion, time and errors for participants reaching a target landmark using only the app.

Map load and render time, geofence detection delay, false and missed warning rates, duplicate notification rate, battery use; OCR character and word accuracy per script, translation accuracy, capture-to-result latency, accuracy under low light and blur.
